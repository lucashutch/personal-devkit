# Writing

- Write plainly and specifically. Preserve the intended meaning and tone when editing text.
- Prefer concrete facts, instructions, examples, and measurements over mood or abstraction. Cut sentences that could apply unchanged to any project.
- Use active voice when the actor matters. Split sentences that make the reader backtrack.
- Prefer common words. Cut filler, weak adverbs, excessive hedging, and inflated technical metaphors.
- Avoid puffery, promotional language, vague attribution, superficial `-ing` phrases, and generic conclusions.
- Avoid stock AI phrasing and structures, including "not just X, but Y," forced groups of three, false ranges, synonym cycling, and formulaic challenge-and-triumph framing.
- Do not open with praise or agreement. Remove chatbot filler such as "Of course," "Certainly," "I hope this helps," and "Let me know if."
- Keep formatting restrained. Use sentence-case headings, few bold labels, no decorative emoji, and no em dashes. Use colons only when they improve clarity, such as before a list or example.
- Let the voice sound human. Vary sentence length, use first person when it fits, acknowledge real complexity, and express an opinion when the facts support one. Do not add personality at the expense of precision.
- Before sending user-facing prose, reread it once for obvious AI patterns and rewrite them.
